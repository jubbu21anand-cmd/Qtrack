How to Use
1
Add people — tap "+ Person" in the header. Each person gets their own bar on the chart and their own card below.
2
Add subjects — on any person's card, tap the Subjects button. Add whatever subjects you study, pick a colour for each, and tap Save. Subjects are shared across all people in your session.
3
Log questions — enter how many you did per subject and tap Add, then tap ✓ Log done at the bottom of the card once you're finished for the day to lock it in.
4
Connect a friend — tap ⚡ in the header, copy your Session ID, and send it. Your friend pastes it in "Join a Session" and hits Join. Both boards sync live.
5
Track progress — use the chart views (Today / By Day / Week / Month / All-Time), the calendar, and the Leaderboard tab. Week view has ‹ › arrows to browse previous weeks; Month view has a dropdown to jump straight to any month.
6
Go anonymous — tap the 🕶 icon on your card to hide your numbers from every other device in the session. Only your own device keeps showing your real progress; everyone else just sees a locked "Anonymous" card. Tap 🕶 again on the same device to turn it back off.
7
Study Tracker — open it from the ☰ menu. Tap a person's name to give them a ring timer. Choose Stopwatch to count up freely, Timer to count down a fixed number of minutes, or Pomodoro for the classic work/break cycle — set your work length and QTrack automatically gives a break (with a long break every 4th round) and flashes it on screen. Several people can run their own timers on the same device at once. Focus time saves automatically to that person's Focus Calendar and weekly chart.
8
Exam Analytics — open it from the ☰ menu. Pick a mode at the top (JEE / NEET / Boards / Other) so competitive and board exam scores never get mixed into the same average. Tap + Add Test, enter total marks, marks obtained, errors, negative marking per wrong answer, a per-subject breakdown (score, rank, percentile), and an optional note. Each saved test becomes its own tab with a summary at the top, and the three charts below track your progress over time, your subject-wise strengths, and your overall subject breakdown for the selected mode.
About QTrack

QTrack is a simple study tracking platform designed to help users organise their work, monitor progress, and keep everything in one place. It supports all subjects and is made to work across devices so your data stays connected wherever you use it.

The goal of QTrack is to provide a clean and straightforward experience without unnecessary complexity. You can track completed questions, manage subjects, log focus time with the Study Tracker, record and analyse exam performance in Exam Analytics, and maintain a clear record of your progress without relying on scattered notes or multiple apps pretending to "increase productivity" while mainly increasing screen time.

QTrack is independently developed with the help of modern AI tools to improve features, usability, and development speed while keeping the platform simple and user-focused.

⚠️ The app is in development phase and may cause bugs and errors. Apologies from the developer's end in advance.

FAQ
What is a Session ID and how do I use it? ▼
A Session ID is a unique code that links multiple devices to the same shared board. Tap ⚡ in the header, copy your Session ID, and send it to your friend. They tap ⚡, paste the ID into "Join a Session" and hit Join. Both of you will see the same data syncing live every 5 seconds. Your session is remembered — you won't need to re-enter it next time.
Can QTrack track questions automatically? ▼
No — QTrack is a manual tracking tool. You enter how many questions you've completed yourself. This is intentional: automatic tracking would require integration with specific apps or platforms, adding complexity and privacy concerns. The manual approach is fast, simple, and works regardless of where you're studying.
Can QTrack connect with other apps? ▼
Currently, no. QTrack does not integrate with third-party apps. All data lives within QTrack's own Firebase database. Integration is on the long-term roadmap but is not available in this version.
How can my friends join my session? ▼
(1) Tap ⚡ in the header. (2) Copy your Session ID. (3) Send it via WhatsApp or any messaging app. (4) Your friend opens QTrack, taps ⚡, pastes the ID and hits Join. They'll immediately see your board and can add themselves as a person.
What does Anonymous mode actually hide, and from whom? ▼
Turning on 🕶 Anonymous for a person hides their question counts, subject breakdown, and chart/leaderboard numbers from every other device connected to the session — those devices just see a locked card confirming "Anonymous mode is on," with no numbers. The device that switched it on keeps seeing that person's full real data, since anonymity is remembered per-device, not per-login (QTrack has no accounts). Only the device that turned it on can turn it back off.
What is the Study Tracker, and what's Pomodoro mode? ▼
It's a focus-time tracker separate from question logging. Pick one or more people from the chip list at the top, and each gets their own ring-shaped timer with a fluid fill/drain animation. Stopwatch counts up freely; Timer counts down a set number of minutes; Pomodoro alternates fixed work sessions with automatic breaks (a short break after each session, a longer break every 4th one), flashing a full-screen banner whenever a session flips from work to break or back. Several people can run independent timers on the same device at once — the rings shrink and re-centre as more people join. Only actual work/focus time is saved to that person's Focus Calendar and 7-day chart; breaks aren't counted.
What is Exam Analytics and why does it have JEE / NEET / Boards modes? ▼
Exam Analytics is where you log full test results — total marks, marks obtained, errors, negative marking, and a per-subject breakdown with score, rank and percentile, plus a personal note per test. Each test appears as its own tab with a quick summary on top. The mode toggle (JEE / NEET / Boards / Other) keeps competitive-exam scores separate from board-exam scores, since JEE-style papers are typically much lower-scoring than board papers — mixing the two would skew your averages and trends. All three modes currently show the same layout; more mode-specific personalisation for competitive vs. board students is planned. Each mode gets its own progress-over-time line chart, a radar chart of subject strengths, and a pie chart of overall subject-wise marks.
